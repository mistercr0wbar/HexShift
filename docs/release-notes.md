# Hex Shift 3

**New in 3.6.0:** The editor opens blank. The getting-started tip is shown once, on a brand-new profile, and an update does not show it again. While it is showing, the picture fits below it. A bottom dock keeps only the height its panels are using, and a panel made shorter than its contents scrolls inside itself. The picture fits to its painted pixels; **View → Artwork bounds** draws that rectangle for the session, does not export it, and does not save a setting. Slider tracks stay a readable length, and the tool strip wraps instead of covering the picture. The magnifier follows the pointer to both edges of the image. Panels can sit beside a neighbour or share a tab strip: drag onto the middle to group them, or onto an edge to place beside; the **…** menu adds Place left, Place right, Place above, Place below, and Move into tabs. **View → Layout** undoes and redoes those arrangement changes for the session. The arrangement itself is still kept on this device as `hexshift-layout`.

**New in 3.5.1:** Three repairs. Variant previews keep the palette they were made from: switching tabs and choosing another palette no longer exports that other palette, and the line above the previews names the set ("Variants: Discord buttons"). Changing or deleting a saved palette does not change variants already made. If the selection, treatment or outline changed, export refreshes those same previews and then saves them. A panel resized very short scrolls inside itself instead of covering the panel below. Column widths you chose are remembered, and a narrower window fits them around the picture instead of squeezing the picture to nothing; widening the window brings the chosen widths back.

**New in 3.5.0:** Panels can be arranged. Each header has a drag grip, the fold control, and a **…** menu: Move to left column, Move to right column, Move to bottom, Move up, Move down, and Hide. Drop a panel on the left column, the right column, or the bottom dock; a marker shows where it will land. Empty columns disappear. Handles resize the columns, the dock, and stacked panels. **View → Panels** shows or hides a panel, and **View → Layout** picks Side columns, Bottom dock, or One right column, locks the arrangement, or resets it. The choice is kept on this device as `hexshift-layout` and is checked when it is read; a file that cannot be read is ignored. It stores no images. On a phone the same menu reorders the sheet's tabs. Reset layout returns to the automatic arrangement, which still follows the window: two columns when it is wide, one column when it is narrower, a dock when it is tall, and the phone sheet when it is small. Folding a panel yourself is remembered; the automatic fold is not. On a short window Swatches folds, then Outline. In one right column Export folds as well, and Selection after that if the column is still too short.

**New in 3.4.0:** Image tabs and batch actions. Open or drop several images together, or paste an image into a new tab. Up to **20 tabs** keep their own colour map, outline, undo/redo, SVG source, zoom/pan, comparison view and variant previews. The untouched sample gives way to the first image you open; an edited sample stays. Click a tab to switch, drag to reorder, and use its × or middle-click to close. Ctrl+Tab / Ctrl+Shift+Tab cycle, Ctrl+1…9 switch directly, and Ctrl+W closes; a phone has a compact image picker. Closing an edited tab or quitting with edits in any tab asks first. **File → Apply to selected tabs / Apply to all tabs** repeats the current tab's colour recipe as one undoable change per tab; Ctrl-click selects tabs and Shift-click selects a range. The source preview is baked once so relative shifts are not applied twice. **Export all tabs…** makes one ZIP using the shared size/format and each tab's outline, with unique filenames. Progress and Cancel sit in the status bar; completed tab changes remain undoable if you cancel. **Batch from files…** still handles up to 100 images without opening tabs. Undo shares one 96 MB budget, trimming background history first, and three recently prepared images are cached for faster switching. Images, tab order and editing history stay in memory; saved colours and themes keep working as before.

**New in 3.3.0:** Saved colours and an in-app colour picker. Click the target, outline or custom preview background colour box for a saturation/brightness square, hue strip, hex and RGB fields, screen picker and Recent row. **+ Save** keeps up to **48 colours** between launches, also shown in Swatches. Click a saved colour to use it, drag to reorder, or right-click/hold for move and remove actions; arrows, Alt+Left/Right, Delete and Shift+F10 provide the keyboard equivalents. **Save as palette…** keeps a named set in **My palettes**; opened palettes offer **Keep in My palettes…**, and `.gpl` import/export still works. Saved data is validated and bounded (48 colours, 50 palettes of 256 colours, 256 KB overall); unreadable data is ignored with a status message. Only colours you explicitly save, palette names/labels and your theme preference are stored. Images and recent targets stay in memory.

A recolouring tool for emoji, icons, stickers and transparent assets: the image in the middle with a menu bar, a colour map, and Selection, Colour, Adjust, Swatches, Outline and Export panels around it, colour themes, magnified picking, image palette, selection brushes, comparison views, exact-colour and transparency modes, palettes, variants, recipes, batch, and ship-ready PNG/WebP/ZIP exports.

Images stay local. The update adds bounded input validation, guarded static SVG import, a restrictive content security policy, cancellable worker processing, scoped offline caches, and tighter Windows-host boundaries. Review docs/security-review.md in the source repository for verification evidence and remaining limitations.

**New in 3.2.0:** Steadier, tighter, and easier to find your way around. The picture no longer jumps while you drag a slider: the status bar used to grow a few pixels each time Hex Shift started working, which shook everything above it, and now nothing moves. On a wide window the controls sit in two columns beside the image instead of a short dock under it, **Selection** on the left and **Colour**, **Adjust**, **Swatches**, **Outline** and **Export** on the right. Each panel has a header you can click to fold it away, and a handle between the columns resizes them. Controls are smaller with a mouse (touch screens keep finger-sized ones), so the hue, chroma and lightness sliders are in view without scrolling; on short windows Swatches and Outline start folded, a click away. The pick, add-sample, brush and pan tools are now a toolbar on the image, with keys I, A, B, E and H. A new **menu bar** (File, Edit, View, Image and Help; Alt or F10 reaches it) puts everything in one place with its shortcuts, including Ctrl+E to export, Ctrl+Shift+C to copy and Ctrl+Enter to apply; **Help** has the shortcuts (F1), these release notes and About, with the version and licences. The **outline** now shows on the image as you set it, can be undone, and is saved in recipes (a recipe saved with one turns it on when applied; recipes without one still open). Exports are unchanged: with the outline off they match 3.1.1 pixel for pixel. In the Windows app Hex Shift now checks for updates every time it starts, about two seconds after the window opens, and again every six hours, and says how it went in the status bar ("Checking for updates…", then "Hex Shift 3.2.0 is up to date"). **Check for updates...** and the switch for automatic checks moved from the title bar's menu to **Help**, and **File > Exit** closes the window, asking first if you have unsaved work. Copies of 3.1.1 still check at most once a day, so for this one update choose **Check for updates...** from the menu you get by right-clicking the title bar. Nothing new is stored.

**New in 3.1.1:** The System theme with the Contrast modifier now boosts your Windows accent colour against black, saturating and brightening it the same way the other themes boost theirs, instead of only brightening it. A grey accent (such as Windows graphite) still shows as white, because grey has no colour to boost.

**New in 3.1.0:** Themes, reworked. The Theme button in the top bar now holds both the theme and a modifier. Five themes: **Default** (neutral graphite), **Lude** (graphite and orange), **Nord**, **Everforest**, and **System**, which follows your Windows accent colour and light or dark app mode and changes as soon as you change them. Each has four modifiers: **Default**, **Day** (light), **Night** (deeper and darker) and **Contrast** (pitch black with the theme colour boosted, or your Windows accent for System), which replaces the separate High contrast theme. Selected buttons now always stand out from the panels behind them, even with a dark accent colour. Your earlier theme choice carries over. The Windows app passes only your accent colour and app mode to the page; nothing new is stored.

**New in 3.0.0:** A new look. The left column of controls is gone: the image now fills the window, with view and zoom controls floating on it, the colour map as a row of chips under it, and **Selection**, **Colour** and **Export** side by side in a dock you can make taller or shorter. Small choices (treatment, colour model, selection view, comparison view, background) are now one-click buttons instead of drop-down lists, text is larger, and icons are drawn rather than font characters. The interface is a neutral graphite by default so only your image is in colour, and the half-circle button beside the light/dark setting offers nine more **colour themes**: High contrast, Lewd, Nord, Solarized, Dracula, Catppuccin, Gruvbox, Rosé Pine and Everforest, each light and dark and each meeting WCAG AA contrast. On phones and narrow windows one panel shows at a time from a tab bar at the bottom; on touch screens controls are finger-sized, two fingers pinch to zoom and pan, and the magnifier sits above your finger. Zooming in past 200% shows each pixel sharp. The app has a new icon: a # split corner to corner in orange and cyan. SVGs exported by Illustrator, Inkscape, Sketch, Figma and icon sets now open (document types with plain entities, reused shapes and symbols, simple stylesheets and editor metadata are handled; scripts, external content and entities that multiply are still refused). Space no longer stops a focused button from being pressed. Your theme choice is still the only thing stored.

**New in 2.5.1 (Windows):** The ◎ button now picks colours from the screen in the Windows app. Since 2.4.0 it did nothing there, because WebView2, the browser engine inside the app, quietly refuses to open the browser's screen picker. The app now has its own: your screens freeze, a magnifier with the colour's hex code follows the mouse, and a click picks the colour. Esc or a right-click cancels, and the arrow keys move one pixel at a time (ten with Shift). Only the picked colour reaches the page; the pictures of your screens are thrown away when the picker closes. In browsers too, cancelling the picker now says "No colour was picked." instead of nothing.

**New in 2.5.0:** Colour map, recipes and batch. The Colour panel now holds a colour map: **+ Add colour** adds a row for each colour you want to change, each with its own selection, treatment and target, all applied together, so swapping red and blue no longer turns both the same colour. **Map all colours** fills the map with the image's colour families in one click, each kept as it is until you set a new colour. Where two changed colours meet, the blended edge pixels are remade from the two new colours, so a recoloured fill and outline meet cleanly, and gradients from one colour into another stay smooth. **Recipes and batch** (in Finish) saves your changes as a recipe file that repeats them on other images, applies a recipe to the open image, or applies your changes to up to 100 images at once into one ZIP at the size and format you chose. Recipes keep colours rather than places, so brush strokes and connected-region limits are left out, and they are read as data with strict size and range limits. Nothing new is stored.

**New in 2.4.1:** Cleaner recolours. Shift colour now gives every shade the target colour's hue, so a red-to-pink heart becomes one even blurple instead of drifting from violet to azure, and the blended pixels along a dark outline shift with the fill, so no rim of the old colour is left around it. Soft fades into other colours inside the artwork stay soft.

**New in 2.4.0:** Palettes, variants and skin tones. The target colour box now takes `rgb()`, `hsl()` and colour names as well as hex, and a new ◎ button picks a colour from anywhere on your screen (Chrome, Edge and the Windows app), such as the real colour of a Discord button. **Palettes and variants** (in Colour) replaces the Discord button list: choose Discord buttons, a 12-colour rainbow at equal brightness, the skin tones of Fluent, Twemoji, Noto or OpenMoji, or a palette of your own: open or paste GIMP, Lospec, JASC or Paint.NET palettes, or any list of hex colours, and save any palette as a `.gpl` file. Skin tones map the light and shadow shades of your selection onto each tone's colours, so a yellow emoji becomes a properly shaded skin tone in one click. **Make a variant for each colour** previews the current edit in every colour of the palette, and **Export all variants** saves them in one ZIP at the size you chose. Palettes are kept only while Hex Shift is open; nothing new is stored.

**New in 2.3.0:** Ship-ready exports. A new Size option in Finish makes files at the exact size each platform wants: Discord emoji and stickers, Slack emoji, a Twitch emote pack (112, 56 and 28 px in one ZIP), Telegram stickers and emoji, a WhatsApp sticker with its tray icon (one ZIP), YouTube emoji, or a custom size. The artwork is centred on a transparent square where the platform needs one, and a readout shows each file's size against the platform's limit before you export. A new small WebP format shrinks a file until it fits (WhatsApp stickers must be under 100 KB); it needs Chrome, Edge, Firefox or the Windows app. PNGs are about 40% smaller (measured on the built-in heart) with every pixel unchanged. SVGs open sharp at 512 px instead of their stated size (Fluent Color emoji were 32 px), with 256, 1024 and 2048 px under **SVG drawn at**, and SVGs with fractional sizes such as `width="32.5"` now open. The Windows app saves ZIP packs through the same Save As checks as images.

**New in 2.2.0:** Truer colour. With shading protection on, the colour you sample now lands exactly on your target (requested and achieved match) while the other shades scale around it. A new Perceptual colour model, on by default, keeps brightness when the hue changes; Classic HSL is one click away. Recolouring white or grey parts now gives one even tint instead of mixed tints. Make transparent gains Clean edges, which removes the colour from blended edge pixels so backgrounds leave no halo. A new Outline option (in Finish) keeps an emoji visible on a background of its own colour, such as a Discord button, and a real-size preview shows the result at 16–96 px on chat and Discord button colours. Large images process far faster: a 2048 × 2048 edit drops from many seconds to well under one.

**New in 2.1.0 (Windows):** Hex Shift can check for updates. At most once a day it asks the project's update address, and if a newer version exists it shows a notice with release notes, **Download update** and **Later**. Nothing is downloaded without a click, and nothing is installed without a second confirmation that reminds you to export your image first. Every update is verified with a cryptographic signature before it can run. Turn the automatic check off, or check by hand, from the window's system menu (right-click the title bar). There is now also a per-user installer, `HexShift-2.1.0-Setup.exe`; the portable program offers it instead of replacing itself. Copies of 2.0 have no updater, so install 2.1.0 once by hand. See docs/updates.md for how it works and how releases are signed.

**Also in 2.1.0:** SVGs with filter effects and blend modes now open, including Microsoft's Fluent Color emoji with their inner shadows. SVG filters that would draw in other images are still refused.

**Windows:** HexShift.exe is portable and currently unsigned. Verify its SHA-256 against SHA256SUMS.txt and its GitHub build provenance before use. A checksum hosted alongside a binary does not independently protect against a compromised publisher account. Do not routinely bypass operating-system warnings. No code-signing identity has been provisioned.

HexShift.html opens locally in a current browser. HexShift.zip includes the executable, HTML, documentation and licence notices.
